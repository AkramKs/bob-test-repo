What Are Apples?

The apple (Malus domestica) is a pomaceous fruit from the rose family (Rosaceae), alongside pears, plums, and cherries. Originating in Central Asia, apples have been cultivated for over 4,000 years and are now grown on every continent except Antarctica.

There are more than 7,500 known cultivars worldwide, ranging from tiny crabapples to large baking varieties. China is the world's largest producer, followed by the United States, Poland, and Turkey.

Popular Apple Varieties

  • Red Delicious — deep red skin, mildly sweet; the classic lunchbox apple.
  • Granny Smith — bright green, tart and crisp; perfect for pies and baking.
  • Fuji — originally from Japan; super sweet, dense, and long-storing.
  • Gala — small, thin-skinned, and honey-sweet; a favorite for fresh eating.
  • Honeycrisp — explosively crisp with a balanced sweet-tart flavor; developed in Minnesota.
  • Golden Delicious — yellow-gold, mellow and buttery; great for sauces and salads.
  • Pink Lady (Cripps Pink) — distinctive pink blush, tangy-sweet, and slow to brown when sliced.

🍎 Fun Apple Facts

  • An apple is about 25% air, which is why they float in water — perfect for bobbing!
  • It takes about 36 apples to produce one gallon of apple cider.
  • The science of apple growing is called pomology.
  • Apple trees take 4–5 years to produce their first fruit and can live for over a century.
  • The largest apple ever recorded weighed 1.849 kg (4 lbs 1 oz), grown in Japan in 2005.
  • There's a common saying: "An apple a day keeps the doctor away" — originating from 19th-century Wales.

Nutrition & Health Benefits

A medium apple (about 180 grams) contains roughly 95 calories, 25 grams of carbohydrates, and 4 grams of fiber — nearly 17% of the recommended daily fiber intake. Apples are rich in vitamin C, potassium, and various antioxidants including quercetin, which has been linked to reduced inflammation and improved heart health.

The majority of fiber and antioxidants are found in the peel, so eating apples unpeeled maximizes nutritional benefit. Studies suggest regular apple consumption may support gut health, lower the risk of type 2 diabetes, and contribute to weight management thanks to their high water and fiber content.
